# Chord progressions as exercises

November 12, 2026. Keith and Claude.

## What it is

Two new entries in the library, **Chord progressions, major** and **Chord progressions, minor**. They play like any other exercise: any key, the ‹ › key stepper, Wait for me, the loop, the games.

- Progressions are stored as Roman numerals, so every one works in all twelve keys. The settings show both the numerals and the chord names in the current key ("I V vi IV · G D Em C, then home to G").
- Major, from rank beginner up: I and V; I and IV; I IV V; I IV I V; I V vi IV (the four-chord song); I vi IV V (fifties); vi IV I V; ii V I; Pachelbel's Canon; twelve-bar blues.
- Minor: i and iv; i and V; i iv V; i VI III VII; i VII VI V (Andalusian); ii° V i. V and vii° take the raised seventh; VII does not.
- Triads only for now, in half notes, two chords a bar. The loop comes home to the tonic at the end.

## Voicing

- **Move least** (the default): each chord in the shape nearest the one before, as pianists play progressions. In C, I V vi IV is C-E-G, B-D-G, C-E-A, C-F-A: the hand hardly moves.
- **Root position**: the root at the bottom every time. The shapes are plain, with jumps between them.

## Hands and fingering

- Right hand: chords around middle C, fingers 1 3 5 (1 2 5 when the root is on top).
- Left hand: chords an octave lower, 5 3 1 (5 2 1 when the fifth is at the bottom).
- Both: chords in the right hand over the root in the left, the root moving to the nearest one each time.
