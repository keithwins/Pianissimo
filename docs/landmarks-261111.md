# Landmarks: reading the staff from a landmark

November 11, 2026. Keith and Claude.

## Why

Reading slowly often means finding each note from scratch, counting lines up or down, even when it is the same note as a moment ago. Landmarks trains the other way of reading: know a few notes at sight, and read the rest as a step or a skip from one you know.

## The game

- One note shows on the staff (treble for the right hand, bass for the left). Play that key.
- It starts with three notes around the clef's own line: G in treble, F in bass. The next note out comes in when the last twelve are nearly all right and typically under 2.5 seconds. The − and + buttons change the number of notes by hand.
- No ledger lines and no sharps or flats yet. Middle C comes later, as the bridge between the staves.
- **Steps** keeps the note before on the staff in grey. The new one is always the same, a step, or a skip from it, so it is read by distance, not by counting from the bottom.
- Notes you miss or are slow on come up more often.
- No clock to beat and nothing to fail. A wrong key shows the right one, with how it relates to the landmark or the grey note, and you play it to go on. Your typical time and your best are shown.
- **Mark the landmark key** lights the landmark on the keyboard picture. Turn it off once you don't need it.

## Next

- Middle C and ledger lines one at a time, then both staves together.
- Answer the step or skip by direction alone (same, up, down), with no note names.
- Chord shapes on the staff: three lines or three spaces in a row, then inversions. This leads into chord progression practice as a kind of exercise.
